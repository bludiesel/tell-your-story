>> Before you start

> the short version

## Why this module exists

Most people who move a gas cylinder have never been shown how. They watched
somebody else do it, that person had watched somebody else, and by the time the
habit reaches you the reason behind it has usually been lost.

That matters because a cylinder is not a heavy object with gas in it. It is a
pressure vessel, and the difference shows up exactly once — at the moment
something goes wrong.

---

> the short version

## What this covers

Four things, in the order you meet them on a normal day: moving a cylinder,
storing it, connecting it, and knowing when to stop and call someone.

None of it is difficult. All of it is the kind of thing that looks like fuss
until the day it isn't.

:::takeaway Before anything else
If you are not sure a cylinder is safe, you do not have to prove it is
dangerous. Put it down, leave it upright, and tell your supervisor.
:::

>> Moving a cylinder

## Where it actually goes wrong

:::opener on the ground
Nearly every injury involving a cylinder happens while somebody is moving it,
and nearly every one of those happens in the last two metres — going through a
doorway, up a kerb, or onto a vehicle. The middle of the journey is rarely the
problem.
:::

---

> keep it upright

## The four rules of moving

A cylinder travels upright, secured, with the valve closed and the cap on. Those
four together are the whole rule, and each one is there because of a specific
failure.

Upright, because liquid reaching the valve is a different substance leaving it.
Secured, because a cylinder that falls will find the one part of you that was
not expecting it. Valve closed, because a knock to an open valve empties the
cylinder in seconds. Cap on, because the cap is the only thing protecting the
valve from the knock.

:::sticky The trolley is not optional {.with-previous}
People skip it for "just across the yard". That crossing is where it lands on a
foot.
:::

---

> when it goes wrong

## What a dropped cylinder actually does

:::compare before
A cylinder dropped with its cap on takes the impact on the cap, stays sealed,
and needs an inspection before it goes back into service.
:::

---

> when it goes wrong

## The same drop, no cap

:::compare after
The same cylinder dropped with the valve exposed can shear the valve off. The
contents leave through a hole the size of a coin, and the cylinder goes with
them.
:::

>> Storing and connecting

## The quiet part of the job

:::opener at rest
A cylinder in storage is doing nothing, which is exactly why storage is where
bad habits accumulate. Nobody is watching, nothing is happening, and the rules
feel theoretical right up until a fire makes them concrete.
:::

---

> the storage rules

## Where a cylinder lives

| Rule | Why it exists |
|---|---|
| Upright, secured, valve closed | the same four rules, at rest |
| Outdoors or in a ventilated store | a leak needs somewhere to go that is not a room |
| Full and empty stored separately | so "empty" is never a guess |
| Away from ignition sources and drains | vapour is heavier than air and travels low |

---

> the storage rules

## Connecting, in order

:::diagram flow
Check the seal | Hand-tighten | Open slowly | Leak-test
:::

Every step earns its place. A perished seal is the most common leak on any site.
Over-tightening deforms the seat and creates the leak it was meant to prevent.
Opening slowly gives you a chance to hear a problem before it becomes one.

:::warning Leak-testing {.step-first}
Soapy water and your eyes. Never a flame, never a lighter, and never the
"it smells fine" test — the odourant fades long before the hazard does.
:::

>> When to stop

> know the line

## The four stop signs

:::marginalia
None of these is a judgement call. Each one is a hard stop: put the cylinder
down, leave the area, and tell somebody. You are not being asked to decide
whether it is serious.

The reason they are absolute is that all four are situations where the next few
seconds matter more than getting it right.

> you do not need to be certain

> "probably fine" is not a category
:::

---

> know the line

## Any one of these

:::big
A hiss, a smell, frost on the valve, or a cylinder you cannot stand upright —
stop, and tell somebody.
:::

---

> the one thing

## If you remember nothing else

Everything in this module reduces to one habit. A cylinder is always upright,
always secured, always capped when it moves, and always somebody else's problem
the moment you are unsure.

:::takeaway The habit
Upright. Secured. Capped. And when in doubt, stop — nobody has ever been
disciplined for putting a cylinder down.
:::
